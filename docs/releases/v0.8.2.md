Fetchrail 0.8.2 adds private local troubleshooting and separately consented reliability analytics, including all v0.8.0 browser-session, scheduling, protected-settings, torrent and interface repairs.

The final-notice production receiver, controlled native canary, signed distribution and live updater are verified in the dated validation record. These notes do not establish legal certification.

## Troubleshoot a download

- The Troubleshoot tab shows bounded local evidence from before a problem is noticed: receiving, retries, intentional limits, writes, verification, attachment processing, publication and saved history.
- Reports distinguish observed evidence, supported hypotheses and unknown causes. Concurrent worker times are not added into a false wall-clock total. HTTP body bytes, checkpoint writes and verified durable completion remain separate observations; torrent selected-ready does not claim HTTP disk durability.
- Imports and browser verification/commit failures have separate local reports, including failures before a download exists. They do not count as admitted fleet downloads.
- Optional protected crash breadcrumbs are off by default, limited to 20 reports/seven days, with inspection and deletion. Unsaved evidence may be lost in a crash.
- Incident JSON requires a preview and review before copying or exporting. There is no automatic incident upload or public incident database.

## Privacy and product health

Existing improvement consent never enables the new product-quality-v2 purpose. Reliability analytics requires a fresh choice for the current notice. Draft choices do not carry forward, and the Privacy screen now checks the native notice revision before showing a grant as current. Declining or withdrawing analytics changes no download functionality.

Eligible new transfers contribute only combined fixed outcome/retry/error/protocol counts, coarse size bands and bounded stage-duration distributions, with public app version and broad OS/architecture. Reports exclude URLs, filenames, paths, credentials, arbitrary messages, exact client event times and user/device/transfer identifiers. Batches stay in memory, are sent at most every 30 minutes, and are discarded on failure or exit. Withdrawal cancels pending work; no history is backfilled.

The protected operator readout preserves release/OS/architecture groups, suppresses small groups and reports sample limits. Intake has per-location burst protection plus shared hourly/daily admission and cardinality caps. The transient provider security key never enters application analytics. These controls cannot establish unique users, representative failure rates or immunity to a distributed attack or account-wide provider exhaustion.

RRMTools is the project operator in Portugal; privacy contact: support.fetchrail@rrmtools.uk. Cloudflare handles transport/security metadata and may process it internationally. Application aggregates last 30 receipt-days; recovery copies may remain up to another 30 days. Absolute anonymity, EU-only processing and regulatory certification are not promised.

## Qualification and limits

Automated fixtures verify known HTTP/torrent failures, consent and withdrawal, strict schemas, protected access, expiry/recovery behavior and output integrity. The final native suites passed 164 test-tools and 149 default-feature tests. Windows, Linux x64/ARM64 and four installed Linux distributions passed the corrected full preflight. Five isolated native profiles verified actual production delivery and withdrawal.

All 120 balanced small cases and eight interleaved 21 GiB outputs passed hash and catalog checks under a 50 MiB/s generated local source. Small comparisons met the provisional 1% median receiving / 2% p95 completion guardrails. Combined large median receiving loss was 0.982%, close to the 1% target; the original subset's 1.207% miss remains included. Four large samples per build do not establish a reliable p95. The older whole-app baseline and source cap do not isolate recorder cost or qualify uncapped/WAN performance. All 800 UI observations completed during active transfers, with candidate p95 frame timing 14.4–15.7 ms through 10,000 history rows. External server/ISP/scanner internals and real storage firmware power-loss behavior can remain unknown.

Packages retain Windows x64 setup and Linux x64/ARM64 AppImage, DEB, RPM and native archives, with existing signature/hash verification and updater metadata. Browser store submission and approval remain separate steps. Existing installation identity, history, browser handoffs and security controls are preserved.
